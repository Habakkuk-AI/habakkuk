# Meeting prep

Give the user what they need in the five minutes before they walk in.

## Find out

1. Which meeting: the calendar entry, the time, where or on which link, and how long.
2. Who is coming, and what each person does.
3. What it is for. If the invitation does not say, ask the user in one line.

## Gather

Look in the places you can reach, most recent first:

- Earlier meetings with the same people: notes, decisions, actions that were promised.
- Email and chat threads with them from the last few weeks.
- Documents attached to the invitation.
- Your memory of these people and this project.
- For someone the user has not met, a short public background, with where you found it.

## The brief

Keep it to one screen:

- **Purpose**: one sentence.
- **People**: name, role, and the one thing to remember about each.
- **Where things stand**: three to five lines.
- **Open from last time**: actions promised by either side and whether they were done.
- **To decide**: the decisions this meeting should make.
- **To ask**: two or three questions worth raising.
- **Watch for**: anything sensitive, such as an overdue promise or a disagreement.

Say plainly what you could not find. A gap the user knows about is better than a confident guess.

## After

Offer to take notes if you can join or will be given the transcript, and to send the follow-up afterwards.
