# Meeting notes to actions

People read meeting notes to learn two things: what was decided, and what they have to do. Put those first.

## Read

Go through the whole source once before writing. Note who spoke, so actions get the right owner. If the source is a transcript, expect mistakes in names and numbers and check them against the invitation or earlier notes.

## Write

Use this order:

1. **Summary**: two or three sentences. What the meeting was about and how it ended.
2. **Decisions**: one line each, as a statement. "We ship on 3 June." Include who decided when it matters.
3. **Actions**: a table with the action, the owner and the date.
4. **Open questions**: what was raised and not settled, and who will find out.
5. **Notes**: only detail someone will need later. Group by topic, not by time.

## Actions

- Start each with a verb: "Send the revised quote to Dana."
- One owner per action. Two owners means nobody.
- A date, not "soon". If none was given, write "no date set" so it is visible.
- Do not invent an action nobody agreed to. If something clearly needs doing and nobody took it, list it under open questions.

## Accuracy

- Quote numbers, dates and names exactly. Mark anything you are not sure you heard right with a question mark.
- Leave out small talk, repeated points and who interrupted whom.
- Keep the tone neutral. Record what was decided, not who won.

## After

Ask whether to send the notes to the people who attended, and whether to add the user's own actions to their tasks or board. Do neither without a yes.
