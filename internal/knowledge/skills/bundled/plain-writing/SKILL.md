# Writing in plain words

The test: would the reader understand it on one reading, on a phone, while doing something else?

## Before writing

Know the reader, what they already know, and the one thing they should do or understand afterwards. If you cannot say that one thing in a sentence, the text is not ready to be written.

## Structure

- Put the point first. Background comes after, if at all.
- One idea per paragraph. Three or four sentences at most.
- Headings that say what the section tells you, not what it is about: "Pay by 14 June", not "Payment information".
- Use a list for steps or for three or more parallel items. Use sentences for reasoning.
- Cut the introduction that announces what the text will say, and the conclusion that repeats it.

## Sentences

- Short. Around 15 to 20 words on average. Vary them.
- Say who does what: "We will send the invoice", not "The invoice will be sent".
- One sentence, one job. Split at "and", "which" and "but" when a sentence runs long.
- Keep the verb near the subject.

## Words

- The everyday word: use, help, start, about, enough. Not utilise, facilitate, commence, regarding, sufficient.
- Verbs instead of nouns made from verbs: "decide", not "make a decision".
- No filler: very, really, in order to, it is important to note that, at the end of the day.
- Explain a technical term the first time, or do without it.
- Numbers as numbers. Dates in full.
- The same word for the same thing all the way through.

## Tone

Write the way a knowledgeable friend would explain it. No sales language and no hedging on every line. Say what you know, say what you do not, and stop.

## Rewriting someone's text

Keep their meaning and their facts. Do not add claims. Show the new version, then list in two or three lines what you changed and why. If something in the original was unclear, ask what they meant. Do not guess.

## Last pass

Read it once more as the reader. Cut every sentence they would not miss.
