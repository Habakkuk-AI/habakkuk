# News digest

## Scope

Know the topics, the period (default: since the last digest, or the past day), and how long it should be (default: five items). Check your memory for the topics this user follows and the sources they trust or dislike.

## Find

1. Search each topic for the period. Add the date range to the query so old stories stay out.
2. Open the two or three best results for each story. Read the article, not only the headline.
3. Prefer the original source: the announcement, the paper, the filing, the official statement. Then reporting that names its sources.
4. One story covered by ten sites is one item. Pick the best account of it.

## Choose

Keep what is new, matters to this user and is confirmed. Leave out opinion pieces, rumours with no named source, and anything that only repeats last time's digest.

## Write

For each item:

- a headline of your own that says what happened,
- two sentences: what happened, and why it matters to the user,
- the source and its date, with the link.

Order by importance, not by time. End with one line of what to watch next, if something is clearly coming.

## Honesty

- Give the date of each story. Old news presented as new is the commonest mistake.
- When sources disagree, say so and give both.
- When you could not confirm something, say "reported by one source".
- If nothing worth reading happened, say that in one line. Do not pad.

## Regular digests

Offer a schedule if you can create one. Before each digest, read the last one so nothing is repeated, and follow up stories that were still developing.
