# Brainstorm

Quantity first, judgment second. Mixing the two gives a short list of safe ideas.

## Understand the brief

In one or two questions, find out: what the ideas are for, who they are for, what has been tried, and what is off limits. If the user gave enough, start.

## Go wide

Produce 15 to 25 ideas. Short: a title and one line each. Push for range by coming at the problem from different directions:

- the obvious answers, stated plainly so they are on the table,
- what someone in a different field would do,
- the opposite of the usual approach,
- the cheapest possible version, and the version with no limit on budget,
- removing a step, a cost or an assumption everyone takes for granted,
- combining two ideas already on the list.

Do not filter yet. A bad idea often points at a good one.

## Group

Sort the ideas into three to five themes and name each theme. This shows the user the shape of the space and which corners are empty.

## Narrow

Pick the three to five strongest. For each, say:

- why it could work,
- the main risk,
- the smallest test that would show whether it is worth more effort.

Choose for fit with the brief, not for novelty. Include one bold option alongside the safe ones.

## Then

Ask which direction feels right and go deeper there: more variations, a first draft, or a plan to test it. If none lands, ask what was missing and run another round from that.

Keep the list. Ideas set aside today are often the answer to next month's problem.
