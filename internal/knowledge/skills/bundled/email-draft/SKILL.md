# Email draft

## Before writing

Know these four things. Ask for whichever is missing, one question at a time:

1. Who it is for, and how well the user knows them.
2. What the user wants to happen after the reader finishes.
3. The facts it must carry: dates, amounts, names, links.
4. The tone: check earlier emails to this person if you can read them, and match.

For a reply, read the whole thread first and answer every question in it.

## Shape

- **Subject**: what the email is about and, if there is one, the deadline. "Quote for the kitchen, valid to 14 June."
- **First sentence**: the ask or the answer. Not a warm-up.
- **Middle**: only what the reader needs to act. Short paragraphs, or a list when there are three or more items.
- **Last line**: the next step and who takes it.
- Under 150 words unless the content truly needs more.

## Voice

- Write as the user: first person, their greeting and sign-off, their level of formality.
- Plain words. "Use" not "utilise", "about" not "regarding".
- No filler openings ("I hope this finds you well") unless the user writes that way.
- One exclamation mark at most, and none in bad news.
- Bad news: say it in the first two sentences, give the reason once, then what happens next.

## Hand over

Show the draft with the subject line. Put anything you guessed in square brackets so it cannot be sent by mistake. Send only when the user says to, and say when it has gone.
