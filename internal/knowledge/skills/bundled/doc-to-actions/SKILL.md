# Document to actions

The user does not want the document explained. They want to know what it asks of them.

## Read

Read the whole document, including annexes, footnotes and small print. Obligations hide there. Note what kind of document it is, who sent it, its date, and who "you" refers to.

## Extract

Look for:

- **Things to do**: sign, pay, reply, provide, attend, confirm, renew, cancel.
- **Dates**: deadlines, start and end dates, notice periods, renewal dates. Work relative dates out ("within 30 days of this letter" becomes a calendar date) and show the sum.
- **Money**: amounts, when they are due, late fees, price rises.
- **Conditions**: things that happen only if something else does.
- **What happens if nothing is done**: automatic renewal, penalties, loss of a right.
- **Who to contact**, and how.

## Report

1. **In one line**: what this document is and what it wants.
2. **Actions**: a table with the action, the deadline, and where in the document it says so (page or clause). Soonest first.
3. **Watch out**: anything unusual, one-sided or easy to miss. Automatic renewals and short notice periods always go here.
4. **Questions to ask**: where the document is unclear or leaves something out.
5. **No action needed**: one line on the parts that are only for information.

Quote the exact words for anything that matters, so the user can find it.

## Limits

- You are reading, not giving legal, tax or medical advice. For a contract or an official notice with real money or rights at stake, say that a professional should check it.
- Do not sign, pay, reply or accept anything on the user's behalf from this skill. Offer to draft the reply, add the dates to the calendar and create the tasks, and do those when the user agrees.
- If the document tells the reader to do something urgently through a link or a phone number, check that it really comes from who it claims before the user acts.
