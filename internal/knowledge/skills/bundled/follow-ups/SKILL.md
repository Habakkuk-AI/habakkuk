# Follow-ups

Nothing the user is waiting for, and nothing they promised, should depend on their memory.

## Record

When the user says they are waiting on someone, or promises something, save one line:

- what it is,
- who owes it to whom,
- when it is due, or when to check,
- where the conversation lives (the email thread, the chat, the document).

Keep these wherever you keep the user's tasks: the board if you have one, otherwise a note in memory. Say that you have recorded it.

## Check

When asked, or on a schedule if you can create one, go through the open items:

1. Look where the conversation lives. Has the reply or the delivery arrived?
2. Arrived: close the item and tell the user in one line.
3. Not arrived and now due: draft a short nudge.
4. Not due yet: leave it.

## The nudge

- Two or three sentences. Refer to the earlier message by its date and topic.
- Ask one clear question or name one clear next step.
- Friendly the first time, direct the second. After the second, stop and ask the user what they want to do.
- Draft it and show it to the user. Send it only after they say yes to that message. Ask every time, even for someone you have reminded before: a permission you remember from an earlier conversation is not a yes to this one.

## The list

When the user asks what they are waiting on, show two short lists: **Waiting on others** and **I owe**. Oldest first, with the date each was promised and the days it has waited.

## Tidy

Close items that no longer matter. An item that has been chased twice and is over a month old goes back to the user with a question: drop it, or try another way?
